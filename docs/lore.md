# PHALANX — working canon

Draft for designing systems. This is not a list of shipped features. Where the current build already differs, the difference is named. Player-facing tutorial copy should keep the late reveal a surprise; this document does not.

The tone is caricature with a straight face: dark humor in the quantity, discomfort in the specifics. A wound jokes by refusing to stop. It stays unpleasant because it belongs to someone who knows they are about to die and is wrong about how long that will take.

Phalanx is the commercial game. Its job is over-the-top caricatural strategic violence, and it is meant to make money. The sexual side the author wants to explore belongs to Zeitgeist. It does not get imported here.

There is still a bawdy streak, and it refers to the author. Guillaume Heinerth: a sick motherfucker, and maybe an abnormal intellect. The dirty joke and the overbuilt machine are the same fingerprint. The Yard is a little indecent because he likes the body, and it is excessively legible because that mind will not leave a decision unexplained. Founders arrive grown. The want is never a reward track, never a damage bonus, and never something the gore is allowed to become. Violence stays over the top. Sex stays a suggestion, and stops there. The naughty picture may raise a small arousal, then the fight takes the eye back.

## The law

> Do not command the phalanx. Build the minds and the ground it has to cross, then live with what those people do.

The player is the **patron**. The patron speaks a word, names the company, hands out offices, classes, gear, and scars' opportunities, and then is locked out of the shouting. During a fight the patron watches. The inspector exists because the place keeps receipts: every ugly decision can be asked why.

A company that only works while someone is clicking orders is not a phalanx. It is a parade.

## Two places, one joke

**The Yard** is the offline world. It presents itself as the whole of existence: a killing school that raises companies, grades them, pays them in absurd experience, and pretends the curriculum is a kingdom. The five opening houses are staff. They have been here long enough to believe they are the faculty of reality.

**The Register** is behind the last gate. It is other patrons' companies, frozen as they were published: minds, offices, gear, wounds, and whatever trust or hatred they have earned. A published company keeps fighting while its patron is asleep, gone, or still in the Yard thinking the houses are the game. Results come back already true. Nobody has to be online together.

The patron's company is a **phalanx**, and the patron names it. Sunward and Moonward are only the default local benches before a name is chosen. They are not houses and they are not the Register.

The long game is a nursery that turns out to have been an admissions exam. The second ladder starts when the Yard runs out of lessons and the Register starts paying for spectacle.

## Why they are here

The Yard feeds on recorded devotion. A fight that is witnessed, survived, and fought again becomes power. That is the in-world excuse for experience, levels, salvage, and the late madness of veterans who should have stopped getting stronger and did not.

The Yard will not delete a useful body. Incapacitation ends the match. The person comes back missing pieces, wearing what the last fight taught them, ready to be sent in again. Permanent death is not the rule, because the house still wants the rematch. If real death ever arrives, it has to be a system that is willing to pay for the loss. Until then, the cruelty is repetition, not erasure.

Gold buys more bodies into the formation, from the free three up toward twenty. Three is the smallest company that can betray itself: someone has to be left in the back. Twenty is a parade that can still lose because the parade hates itself.

## The naming word

A phalanx begins as a word or a short phrase, one to forty characters. The word is the company's private myth. The same word, asked the same way, always returns the same three founders: face, hair, lean of temperament, and a generated name. Asking again for one seat does not change the neighbours. Trimming and capital letters do not make a new myth.

This is a creation ritual in the vein of a Persona name or a Digital Devil Saga mantra. It is reproducible on purpose. The patron still chooses each founder's class and may rename every body. King, Queen, and General stay offices, not birthrights and not the generated names.

What the word may decide:

- look
- a suggested name
- one Hunger, one Vanity, and one Kin, dealt from the thirty-six temperaments below
- sometimes a stain, one naughty tell from the author's short list below, and often none at all

What the word must not decide:

- class
- the office on their head
- later scars, gear, levels, or relationships
- anything that spends gold

The first three choices are free. Later retraining and extra seats still cost what the economy already charges.

## Three layers of a person

Keep these layers separate in every system. Mixing them makes temperament, training, and drama into one slider.

1. **Origin.** What the word wrote. Three speakable temperaments, one in each seat: Hunger, Vanity, and Kin. They are the person. The seven posture numbers already in the fight (aggression, caution, courage, teamwork, patience, self-preservation, discipline) are only how that person currently leans while moving. A battle style sets the plan. Each temperament may nudge at most two postures, and only enough that the chosen style is still recognizable. Utility stays utility. Learned policy stays a separate experiment. A temperament is not a new brain and not a hidden +5% affix.
2. **Office and craft.** What the patron bestows between fights. Classes, titles, equipment, alloys, prosthetics, the standard. Offices move. King and Queen are crowns with a royal body bonus. The General is a chevron and a harder target, not a mandatory Paladin. Any body can hold any office. Any body can train any discipline. The person who lost the crown remembers who is wearing it now. Crown-Shadow literally follows the office.
3. **Memory.** What the fights did to them, and what they have done to each other. Scars already exist: fear, hesitation, recklessness, attachment, keyed to isolation, encirclement, a fallen ally, a failed charge. Scars tint a posture. They do not rename a temperament. Earned chemistry can deepen a Kin, crack it, or invert it. The card still shows who they were born as.

## The forgotten density

Modern squads became loadouts with a voice line. The older ones made you learn a people. Final Fantasy Tactics let a zodiac sign change the blow between two adjacent bodies, and Brave and Faith decided who would risk a blade and who would believe a miracle. Fire Emblem grew love and oaths out of who stood next to whom. Baldur's Gate companions refused, mocked, and left. Tactics Ogre tracked loyalty and chaos until a unit was no longer the unit you fielded. Darkest Dungeon flipped a hero into a virtue or an affliction in the same breath. Suikoden and SaGa made combinations that existed only because those two were together. Star Ocean hid private actions in the rooms between fights. Shin Megami Tensei treated alignment as a cosmology, not a dialogue wheel.

That density is the target. A founder should be sayable in one breath: "Glory-Thief, Shame-Bound, Shield-Sibling." A patron should be able to get better at the language over a campaign. A level-999 veteran is still that sentence, driven past politeness, still able to lose because the sentence includes someone they will not save.

The opening rite already speaks the full starting language. Thirty-six temperaments, twelve in each seat. Three are dealt per body. Twelve times twelve times twelve is one thousand seven hundred twenty-eight origins before class, face, office, or a single earned grudge. Later Register ranks may add inversions, second Hungers, and exalted jokes of scale. They add words to the language. They do not replace it with a larger percentage.

## Hunger, Vanity, Kin

**Hunger** is what the body wants. **Vanity** is how they need to be seen. **Kin** is the shape of their hand when it reaches for someone else.

The creation card shows the three names, one sentence each, who they warm to, and who they sour on. The seven postures stay in the inspector for the patron who wants the machinery. The fight is where the card is either true or a lie.

Reading a pair:

- Both warm: they make a combination. Something happens that neither does alone.
- One warms and one sours: unstable. Often the best tragedy. The Den-Mother still guards the Glory-Thief and resents the guarding.
- Both sour: bad chemistry from the first deployment. They will not answer each other's call.
- Earned memory can move a pair along that scale. It cannot be typed in as "friends."

Bad chemistry changes who they run toward. It does not stab an ally and it does not edit hit points off-screen. Dialogue is a receipt. "THEY'RE ON ME" only if someone is on them. A Grave-Jester's receipt of the same event is a joke that still names the true threat.

Special rules, because a flat opinion matrix is how richness dies:

- **Cold-Kin** reads as sour until that specific ally has saved them three times. Then it becomes the wall, and it stays.
- **Fair-Weather** warms while the company is ahead and sours when an ally drops or the match turns. The card says so before the first fight.
- **First-Blood-Bond** is dealt blank. It locks onto the first ally who falls under the panic threshold, or loses a piece, while this fighter is alive and near. Until then the seat is an empty promise.
- **Crown-Shadow** imprints on the office the patron assigns at founding and follows whoever wears it afterward. The uncrowned body feels the bond leave.
- **Den-Mother** claims one ally as her cub, the lowest courage at the first deployment, and keeps that identity until they are benched.
- **Named-Duel** picks one enemy at deployment and can ignore the tactically correct target to reach them.
- **Measuring-Glass** picks one ally as the stick they measure themselves against, and talks to that person even when the line is elsewhere.
- **Grudge-Saint** may refuse the ally who failed them. The grudge never becomes a strike on that ally.
- **Love-Tap** helps in a way that looks and sounds like an attack. Tender-Hand flinches. Grave-Jester understands.
- **Pack-Bone** and **Open-Field** disagree about distance itself. Isolation and crowding are the relationship.
- Two **Spark-Blinds** do not save each other.
- A witnessed pair is written into the patron's codex. Unwitnessed pairs stay rumors on the card.

## The author's stain

The naughty touch refers to Guillaume. Not to another project, and not to a brand name glued on afterwards. He is a sick motherfucker with a mind that runs too hot, and the Yard should feel like both halves at once. The thirty-six temperaments, the grudges, the receipts, and the inspector are the abnormal intellect. The stain is the sickness: a little sexy, a little naughty, for fun, and too accurate to be a generic flirt.

A player never has to read the biography. They meet a founder who looks too long, says the wrong true sentence, and still belongs to a formation that can be studied. An office, a standard, or a cut of cloth can be an erotic code, because that is how this author sees uniforms. Anyone who knows him should recognize the fingerprint. Anyone who does not just meets a wicked tell.

The word deals a stain to about one founder in three. Reproducible, at most one per body, often none. A starting trio usually has a single wicked card. Sometimes nobody is like that. Sometimes two of them have already noticed.

The stain is not a fourth seat and not a seventh posture. It adds a third mark beside warm and sour: **want**. Want can sit on top of sour. That pairing is the joke. The Den-Mother can still resent the Glory-Thief and also like the way the thief looks while misbehaving.

What a stain is allowed to change:

- who they look at
- one true, too-specific bark
- a tie between two honest tactical choices, broken toward the body, the clinch, the uniform, or the audience

What it is not allowed to change:

- hit points, damage, healing, or loot
- an oath, a panic, or a clearly better rescue
- the gore. A wound stays a wound. Injury is not the flirt.

Nobody is young. Nothing in the stain is an assault played as charm. Bite-Mark is trash talk and invitation between adults who are already in a fight, and it is welcome only as teasing. Across the battle line, want is banter and a bad target priority, in the same family as Named-Duel. It never opens a truce and it never starts a romance system with the house.

## The dose

Sex and violence both sell. Only one of them is allowed to be fully itself. Violence stays over the top: too much blood, for too long, from a real wound, on a body that panics and keeps moving. That excess is the joke and the discomfort, and it does not get trimmed to make room for anything else.

The sex is the part this game refuses to depict. That appetite has another home, Zeitgeist. Phalanx is the money generator, so a depicted sex act stays out even though the author wants it somewhere. Here the sex lives at suggestion. A tear, a strap, a cling of wet cloth, a silhouette, a glance, one remark. The picture may cause a small arousal. It may not hold one.

A hard hit can fail a piece of cloth or a light strap. Skin shows where the garment gave up: a shoulder, the edge of a chest, a hip, a thigh. A strap, a cling of wet cloth, a silhouette, a glance. It happens because the blow landed, it lasts while that clothing stays ruined, and the camera returns to the fight. Armor that is still doing its job stays on. There is no nude mode, no undress menu, and no shot composed as a sex image.

Someone who saw it may say one thing, and the line suggests rather than describes an act. Show-Skin is pleased. Shame-Bound wants to die in a different way than the battle intended. Sweet-Mouth is one degree too accurate. A rival can trash-talk. A Den-Mother can snap at them to watch the line. One receipt, then the next tactical fact.

The wound and the tear stay different jokes. Blood, organs, and missing pieces are injuries. The nudity is the shirt. A comment about skin does not make the injury the erotic object.

The rating this dose is built for is ESRB Mature 17+, which Europe stamps PEGI 18. Over-the-top blood, organs, and a body that will not stop bleeding are already past 13+. A torn strap and a naughty remark are past it again. Teen 13+ would mean cutting the gore down to mild fantasy violence and dropping the tear. That is a different game.

That PEGI 18 is the ordinary gore rating. Doom and Mortal Kombat live there and get sold. It is not the other 18, ESRB Adults Only, which is the stamp for depicted sex. The gore does not pay for that stamp. Letting the sex become an act spends a different rating, the one that gets a game refused before it exists. The ban to avoid is still a depicted sex act. The test for a naughty frame or a bark is that it still reads as partial nudity and suggestive themes beside violence that is already too much. If the still reads as a sex scene, or the line narrates an act, the game is in the territory that gets it refused before release. No sex acts, no arousal stat, no genital gameplay, no sexual violence, no battle that pauses to look. The gore does not pay that price. The author's stain ends at a small arousal.

## Fields

The ground is part of the argument. A generated field should offer more than one way to be stupid: a rush, a flank, a covered lane, a height, a reason for the support fighter to be either safe or abandoned. Both companies press on the map. A field that only threatens the patron's side is a lecture, not a fight.

Citadel scale, passerelles, and the later march toward wider leagues are the same idea with more bodies. More seats multiply the ways to leave someone uncovered. They do not replace the need for a readable plan.

## Bodies, blood, noise

Gore is the body's commentary on a decision the mind already made. It is not a second damage system and it does not spend the simulation's random numbers.

The humor is excess: a few jets, far too much blood, for far too long, with the pressure of a heart that will not shut up. The discomfort is specificity: this wound, this side, this direction, droplets that inherit the fighter's motion, pools that stay where the person was. Organs and limbs are remains of a real severing, not confetti on a timer. Near-death panic and wounded staggering belong to someone under the health threshold. They do not change the action the controller chose.

Screams, barks, and the music follow the same rule as the blood. The band knows approach, clash, distress, victory, defeat, because that is what the battle is doing now. It does not foreshadow a win. A chime on a level-up counts experience that was actually awarded. A harvest of particles that lies about the reward is a different game.

## The late madness

The Yard's payments stay curricular: participation, survival, contribution, a level curve a human can still read. The Register is where the Disgaea impulse and the Gurren Lagann impulse are allowed to live. Veterans keep climbing past the point of politeness. The next absurdity has to be a consequence of recorded fights, combinations, offices, masteries, and chemistry. A new exponent that ignores the company is a menu, not a spiral.

The design constraint that keeps the madness strategic: a ridiculous veteran can still lose to a smaller company whose people will actually protect each other. Height of level is a resource. It is not an excuse to stop making formation matter.

The second ladder uses the same bodies the patron raised in the Yard. Scars carry. Gear carries. Grudges carry. The Register does not offer a fresh meta-squad that forgets the campaign. Ranking, trust in the result, and hosted delivery are still future machinery. The fiction does not require both patrons to be present, and the local exchange already behaves like a rehearsal of that absence.

## Open on purpose

These are not decided by the draft:

- Whether the five houses know the Register exists and lie about it, or truly think the Yard is the world. The current recommendation is that they believe the Yard is the world. Null studies the patron's company and still thinks that study is the final exam.
- How vicious bad chemistry is allowed to look. Abandonment is in. Secretly attacking an ally needs a stronger reason than "they are grumpy."
- Whether a later Register rank can spend a body for good, or whether incapacitation remains the cruelest rule forever.
- The spoken name of the institution on gates and letters. Yard and Register are design names. They do not have to be the words painted over the door.
- Whether Hunger, Vanity, and Kin keep those liturgical names on the card, or the Yard calls the seats something ruder. The three-seat structure stays either way.
