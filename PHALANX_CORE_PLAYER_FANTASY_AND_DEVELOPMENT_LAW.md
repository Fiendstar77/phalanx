# PHALANX — Core Player-Fantasy and Development Law

## Purpose

PHALANX must become a game people can play for years because the simulation keeps producing new consequences, new relationships, new discoveries and new stories.

The goal is not simply to build an autonomous combat game.

The goal is to make the player become deeply attached to a persistent Phalanx whose members develop through actual experience.

The core fantasy is:

> **Build them. Watch them fight. Watch them change. Eventually watch them become legendary.**

---

# 1. PLAY IS THE REAL CURRENCY

PHALANX must never make purchased power more valuable than time, understanding or history.

The deepest rewards must come from:

- playing,
- experimenting,
- surviving,
- losing,
- adapting,
- learning the systems,
- building combinations,
- developing veterans,
- and accumulating meaningful history.

Real-money monetization, if used, should be cosmetic.

Money may buy:

- appearances,
- armor skins,
- weapon skins,
- banners,
- heraldry,
- hairstyles,
- visual effects,
- poses,
- cosmetic presentation.

Money must not buy:

- stronger fighters,
- better attributes,
- class power,
- veteran status,
- legendary events,
- combat advantages,
- psychological development,
- progression shortcuts that undermine earned history.

Core law:

> **Money buys expression. Play buys meaning.**

---

# 2. THE PLAYER MUST BECOME ATTACHED TO INDIVIDUAL FIGHTERS

A fighter must eventually become much more than:

```text
CLASS + LEVEL + EQUIPMENT
```

A veteran should accumulate:

```text
IDENTITY
+ ATTRIBUTES
+ SKILLS
+ CLASS MASTERY
+ EQUIPMENT
+ INJURIES
+ PROSTHETICS
+ PERSONALITY
+ PSYCHOLOGICAL SCARS
+ CONFIDENCE
+ RELATIONSHIPS
+ HABITS
+ COMBAT HISTORY
+ TITLES
+ LEGENDARY EVENTS
```

Two fighters with similar statistics should still become fundamentally different because they experienced different lives.

The desired player reaction is:

> “No. I am not replacing her. She has been with me since the beginning.”

That attachment is one of the primary products of the simulation.

---

# 3. PREDICTABLE RULES, UNPREDICTABLE LIVES

PHALANX should not depend on arbitrary scripted spectacle.

The rules should remain understandable and inspectable.

The lives generated by those rules should be unpredictable.

The player should understand why something was possible while still being surprised that it happened.

Core law:

> **Predictable rules. Unpredictable lives.**

---

# 4. THEUS IS THE SHARED RULE FOUNDATION

Theus defines capability and consequence.

PHALANX defines autonomous behavior under pressure.

Conceptually:

```text
THEUS
WHO IS THIS PERSON?
WHAT CAN THEY DO?
WHAT HAPPENS WHEN THEY TRY?

PHALANX
WHAT DO THEY CHOOSE TO DO?
WHY?
HOW DOES THE EXPERIENCE CHANGE THEM?
```

Theus should remain suitable as a shared framework for future games such as Zeitgeist.

Its purpose is not to reproduce GURPS or Rolemaster.

Its purpose is to provide an original universal framework inspired by useful ideas from both:

```text
GURPS-LIKE STRENGTH
→ broad character modelling

ROLEMASTER-LIKE STRENGTH
→ detailed consequences and critical outcomes

THEUS
→ original unified implementation for our games
```

---

# 5. LEGENDARY ACTS MUST EMERGE FROM THE SIMULATION

PHALANX fighters must eventually be capable of performing extraordinary actions.

The target player reaction is:

> “OHHHHHHHH WHAT THE FUCK DID HE JUST DO?!”

These moments must not simply be canned ultimate abilities.

A fighter should be capable of composing legal actions from real capabilities, terrain and opportunity.

Example:

```text
SPRINT
→ WALL STEP
→ VAULT
→ LAND ON ELEVATION
→ INTERCEPT
→ STRIKE
→ LAUNCH ENEMY
```

Another:

```text
ALLY IN CRITICAL DANGER
+
STRONG RELATIONSHIP
+
HIGH COURAGE
+
AVAILABLE ROUTE
+
ENOUGH EFFORT
+
RELEVANT SKILL

→ fighter accepts extraordinary risk
→ crosses dangerous terrain
→ intercepts attacker
→ saves ally
→ possibly suffers serious injury
```

The system should be capable of producing acts that look authored even though they emerged from lawful simulation.

---

# 6. OPPORTUNITY COMPOSITION

Long term, PHALANX should support a layer above ordinary action selection.

Normal actions remain atomic:

```text
MOVE
ATTACK
DEFEND
SUPPORT
RETREAT
CAST
REPOSITION
```

Exceptional behavior may compose several legal primitives into a short tactical sequence.

Conceptually:

```text
SITUATION
↓
AVAILABLE ACTION PRIMITIVES
↓
SKILL / ATTRIBUTE REQUIREMENTS
↓
TERRAIN OPPORTUNITIES
↓
EFFORT COST
↓
RISK TOLERANCE
↓
PSYCHOLOGICAL / RELATIONSHIP PRESSURE
↓
SHORT COMPOSED PLAN
↓
EXECUTION
```

This must not bypass simulation authority.

Every sub-action must remain legal.

The fighter does not receive magic permission simply because a move would look cool.

---

# 7. EXTRAORDINARY ACTIONS MUST HAVE REQUIREMENTS AND COSTS

Example:

```text
VAULTING STRIKE

Requirements:
- sufficient Agility
- sufficient Acrobatics
- usable leg
- enough Effort
- valid surface
- reachable destination
- legal target
- valid weapon state

Influences:
- injury
- fear
- confidence
- momentum
- terrain
- weapon
- enemy awareness
- fatigue
- relationship pressure
```

Success should not always produce the same animation or consequence.

Theus resolution can determine whether the result becomes:

```text
clean success
partial success
critical success
bad landing
weapon break
collision
enemy launch
self injury
mutual collapse
catastrophic failure
```

The spectacle comes from consequence, not from an animation button.

---

# 8. RELATIONSHIPS SHOULD ALTER RISK

Relationships should eventually affect tactical decisions.

A fighter may attempt something they would normally reject when:

- a bonded ally is about to fall,
- a rival is vulnerable,
- a commander is threatened,
- someone they protect is isolated,
- a previous trauma is being repeated.

This allows personality and history to produce dramatic exceptions without making behavior random.

Example:

```text
NORMAL RISK TOLERANCE
= reject dangerous maneuver

BUT

bond_to_ally = very high
ally_health = critical
courage = high
reachable_intervention = true

→ temporary willingness to accept extreme risk
```

---

# 9. HISTORY MUST RECORD LEGENDARY MOMENTS

PHALANX should eventually detect and record exceptional events.

This should not be a generic achievement system.

It should be a history system.

Example:

```text
THE FALL AT BLACK BRIDGE

Mira crossed 18 metres under ranged fire while critically wounded,
vaulted the western barricade,
intercepted an attack against Halden,
launched the attacker from the bridge,
and survived with 3 HP.
```

That event may become part of Mira's permanent history.

Possible derived title:

```text
Mira "Bridgebreaker"
```

Titles do not need mechanical bonuses.

The meaning itself is valuable.

---

# 10. LEGEND MUST BE EARNED, NOT ASSIGNED

Characters should not begin as legendary heroes.

They become legendary because of what actually happened.

Core law:

> **Characters do not have legendary status because the game says they do. They become legendary because the simulation has evidence that they did legendary things.**

Possible signals for a legendary event:

- extreme survival odds,
- severe injury,
- unusually high-value intervention,
- multiple successful actions in sequence,
- saving another fighter,
- defeating several opponents,
- unusual terrain traversal,
- critical success under exhaustion,
- winning while heavily outnumbered,
- extraordinary comeback,
- surviving a severe critical,
- major relationship-triggered intervention.

Do not reduce this to a single arbitrary score too early.

Record the underlying receipts first.

---

# 11. TELEMETRY MUST SUPPORT STORY

Combat telemetry should not exist only for research.

It should eventually support persistent narrative reconstruction.

Useful receipts include:

```text
who
what
where
when
against whom
health state
injuries
effort
skills used
terrain interaction
relationship context
risk level
result
damage
survival
battle importance
sequence of actions
```

This allows PHALANX to reconstruct meaningful history from real events instead of fabricating prose disconnected from the simulation.

---

# 12. EVERY SYSTEM SHOULD CREATE FUTURE CAUSES

When evaluating a new feature, ask:

> **Does this create new meaningful causes for future behavior?**

Weak implementation:

```text
injury
→ -10 HP
```

Strong implementation:

```text
injury
→ movement changes
→ weapon limitations
→ psychological reaction
→ equipment adaptation
→ class implications
→ ally behavior changes
→ future tactical choices
```

The same principle applies to:

- equipment,
- classes,
- skills,
- relationships,
- psychology,
- terrain,
- titles,
- crafting,
- prosthetics,
- legendary events.

Systems should interact rather than exist as isolated bonuses.

---

# 13. DEEP PLAY SHOULD REVEAL MORE DEPTH

A new player should understand basic interactions immediately.

A veteran player should still discover new interactions hundreds of hours later.

Desired learning curve:

```text
10 HOURS
"Guardian protects Ranger."

50 HOURS
"This temperament works better with this formation."

200 HOURS
"Her isolation scar changes retreat behavior, so this equipment compensates."

1000 HOURS
"I know exactly why these four fighters work together."
```

The complexity should emerge from interaction.

Do not require the player to understand everything before they can enjoy the game.

---

# 14. LONG-TERM PLAYER IDENTITY

The player's true possession is not merely equipment.

It is their Phalanx.

An asynchronous opponent should eventually be inspectable as history:

```text
KORRIN "THREE GRAVES"

612 battles
left arm replaced
Guardian / Warrior mastery
31 recorded legendary events
fear: enclosed spaces
bond: Ysera
favoured weapon: war axe
notable event: survived the Black Quarry encirclement
```

The reaction should be:

> “Oh shit. I am fighting THAT guy.”

The opponent's history should matter emotionally even before combat begins.

---

# 15. CONTENT IS NOT THE PRIMARY SOURCE OF REPLAYABILITY

PHALANX should not rely on constantly producing authored content faster than players consume it.

Core distinction:

```text
CONTENT YOU CONSUME
eventually runs out

SYSTEMS YOU INHABIT
keep producing consequences
```

Classes, weapons, maps and campaigns provide inputs.

The persistent simulation produces the long-term fascination.

---

# 16. COMMERCIAL PRINCIPLE

PHALANX does not need AAA presentation to justify itself.

The priority is:

```text
SYSTEMIC DEPTH
> BEHAVIORAL BELIEVABILITY
> PLAYER ATTACHMENT
> READABILITY
> PRESENTATION POLISH
```

Visual quality matters, but systemic depth is the thing that can make the game exceptional.

The desired reaction to a low-priced release is:

> “How the hell is there this much game underneath this?”

The game should create goodwill by delivering disproportionately deep systems rather than extracting maximum spending.

---

# 17. FINAL DEVELOPMENT LAW

PHALANX should make players feel that they are raising a fighting organism.

The player shapes it.

The world pressures it.

Theus determines what is possible and what consequences follow.

The fighters act autonomously.

History changes them.

Relationships alter them.

Injuries alter them.

Success and failure alter them.

Occasionally everything aligns and one of them does something completely insane.

And because the player knows it was not a scripted cinematic, the moment matters.

The ultimate PHALANX fantasy is:

> **This is my team.**
>
> **These are my veterans.**
>
> **I know what they survived.**
>
> **I know why they fight the way they do.**
>
> **And I still do not know what the little maniacs are going to do next.**
